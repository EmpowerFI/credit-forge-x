# Demo script: one economic loop

Two scripts, one story. The ninety-second version answers the question the whole product exists for — global capital reached a business, and then what — and needs no wallet, no signature and no live run. The full walkthrough follows the same story end to end: a sponsor's evidence, credit intelligence, a qualified opportunity, capital, repayment, circulation in the territory, and outcome, then back to the sponsor.

It runs on the hackathon environment at `www.empowerfi.io/app`. The login page enters each demo account with one click — nobody types a password on camera.

The bar at the top of the app numbers the two stories: 1 Impact Intelligence, 2 Investor Console. Operations sit beside them. **The Credit & Capital Engine is in no menu at all**, by design: the database allocates the moment a qualified request opens to investors, so the decision is already taken before anyone could click anything, and a panel offered before the thing it explains reads as a step the product makes you take. It is reached from the money instead — from a position, under *Where the money went*, as **Why this opportunity existed**, which opens it on that request. On the right, **View platform as** switches between the five views (sponsor, investor or impact fund, operator, community, entrepreneur): each opens a page with that view's value and tools. A demo account switches to the right demo persona in one click, so a judge never has to sign out.

> Prototype of a future regulated P2P productive-credit architecture. Hackathon investments, returns, FX and Pix settlement are simulated; blockchain transactions use test assets on Devnet.

## Ninety seconds, if that is all there is

The short version answers one question: global capital reached a business — and then what? It is three button presses after the entry, because the argument is a movement and a movement has to be watched rather than read. It starts where the money is rather than at a panel: click **2 Investor Console** in the bar (it enters as the demo investor), open *Portfolio* → her position on the global loan that is already repaying, and from *Where the money went* click **Why this opportunity existed**. The engine opens on that very request. Stay there until the last beat.

- **"Is any of this real?"** The evidence mark in any page header opens **What every number here is made of**: eleven families of figures, each labelled from its own rows. **Optional, after step 9 (40 s) — the Zcash track:** the same investment, paid privately, and then handed to the auditor to check without us.

*The payment.* In the invest panel choose **ZEC blindado** instead of **USDC da Devnet**. No wallet is connected and none is needed. The panel answers with EmpowerFI's shielded address, the amount in ZEC at the quote it just locked, a memo carrying a random reference, and a QR. Pay it from any Zcash wallet. The four steps then move on their own: *paid* → *seen in a Zcash block* → *carried into the vault, in a batch* → *allocated and proven on Solana*. "She has no Solana wallet, and she does not need one. She pays a shielded address on Zcash. The amount, the memo, the sender and the recipient are all encrypted — and what reaches Solana is the operator crediting the vault, so no investor wallet is ever linked to that inflow."

*The batch, which is the point.* Step three names the crowd: *"Your capital entered with 3 other investors, in one movement of $40.00. That number is nobody's amount, so Solana shows neither your position nor your total."* Say why it had to exist: **"Her amount was unreadable on Zcash, and then we credited the vault with her exact amount a minute later. The envelope was sealed and its shadow gave it away. So credits are carried in batches, rounded down to whole units, and the rest waits for the next one — the number on Solana is the sum of nobody. We round *down*, never up: the operator never moves money it has not received, so the vault is short by less than one unit between batches, and that float is published on the audit page rather than hidden."**

If the batch happens to carry one investor, the screen says it hides nothing, and that is the honest line to read aloud rather than skip — *a batch of one is not a crowd*. To avoid it, seed the payers first with `scripts/platform/seed-zcash-batch.mts`, which previews what the batch will do and spends no testnet ZEC until `--yes`. `--amounts 13000000,8000000` picks how many and how much.

**Pay them in one transaction, or you get a batch each.** Seeding several payers is not enough: `zcash-watch` runs every minute and closes one batch per run, and shielded change needs confirmations before it can be spent, so paying the requests one after another puts each one a minute apart — and each lands in a batch of its own. Four payers, four batches of one, which is the case the screen calls out as hiding nobody. ZIP 321 carries several payments in one transaction, so send them together and the receipts arrive in the same pass of the watcher:

```
zcash:?address=<treasury>&amount=0.00971&memo=<memo1>&message=EmpowerFI%20<ref1>&address.1=<treasury>&amount.1=0.00598&memo.1=<memo2>&message.1=EmpowerFI%20<ref2>
```

One payment has no index, the next is `.1`, and so on — all on one line, with no
line break inside the URI.


Pass that to `zcash-devtool wallet -w <wallet> pay --payment-uri "<uri>"` — the amounts, memos and references are the ones the seed script printed. On 2 Oct 2026 this produced one batch of two investors: `$4 carried in + $13 + $8 = $25 → the vault sees $20, $5 waits`. The alternative is to stop the `zcash-watch` cron while the payments land and start it again after, which needs database access the demo machine may not have.

*The check.* **Operations → Audit → Zcash**. The treasury's **viewing key**, with **Reveal** and **Copy**, and under it the four commands that import it into `zcash-devtool` and list the same payments. The `enhance` step is not optional: without it `list-tx` reports `0 memos` and no `EFI-` reference, which reads as if the references were never on chain. "We can read our own treasury because we hold its viewing key. So can an auditor — here it is, and here is how to list these same notes without trusting anything on this screen. It reads everything and can spend nothing. That is selective disclosure, not 'trust our dashboard'."

*If a judge clicks the explorer link and lands on "Transaction Not Found",* that is the demonstration, not a fault, and the screen says so beside the link: a shielded transaction shows no amount, no memo and no addresses, an explorer can only say one exists, and testnet explorers often lag or skip the newest shielded pool. The block height beside it came from our own watcher, which reads blocks and never the mempool — a note it recorded is a note that was in a block.

*Timing.* Testnet block intervals are irregular: a payment has taken 3 minutes and it can take longer. Pay ten minutes before recording and show the finished state, or pay live and cut to the audit console while it confirms. Do not stand in front of the four steps waiting.

It hands each investor's *historical* assets to a wallet derived from the operator's secret; the mint cron then creates them on devnet, five a minute, so a set of 40 takes about eight minutes. Those assets are what the email sessions show, by investor rather than by wallet.
  - **Nothing in the demo requires knowing a wallet address in advance.** Step 9 is signed in *with the wallet*, which is its own account: signing in with Solana makes a capital provider known by its address, and `record_investment` admits that address from the deposit the chain has already proved. So the asset in step 13 mints to whatever wallet the presenter — or a judge reproducing this — happens to connect, and the destinations offered in step 14 are the seeded funds' own wallets. The `--investor-wallet` flag still exists for handing the seeded portfolio to a wallet you hold, which shows *held, funded by another* from the wallet's own side. It is a nicety and no step depends on it.
  - Proofs take about half an hour to confirm on devnet after a reseed.
- **Settlement cases** are seeded on global loans and appear in the demo investor's portfolio: two of the direct route, one of the stablecoin winning, one of the stablecoin unavailable. The seed prints them at the end of a run.
- **Case codes** (Q-…) change with every reseed. The engine's picker searches by code, purpose and amount.
- **The local rail** is seeded in one territory only, Grajaú, and the seed prints what it holds: what was injected, what was traded inside the territory, what was cashed out, and the multiplier, retention and velocity — all read back from the Local Economy dashboard rather than counted twice. The run is deterministic and warns if only one loan lands on the rail where a full run lands two; that warning now means something changed in how the desk picks, not that it is worth reseeding and hoping.
- **The backing line the ninety seconds reads out loud** is printed by the same run: what went in, what was released to investors, what merchants cashed out, what is left, and whether it still covers what circulates. Rehearse beat 5 from that line, not from this document — those four figures move with the book, and a rehearsed number that has quietly drifted is worse on camera than no number at all. If it ever prints **NOT COVERED**, stop: the rail is claiming more units than it holds reais for, which is the one thing the screen must never be caught saying.
- **For the Zcash optional:** a Zcash **testnet** wallet with a little TAZ (fauzec.com gives 1 TAZ per address per 24 h, and accepts a `utest1…` unified address; the jinolabs faucet this doc used to name stopped answering by 2 Oct 2026), and the treasury's viewing key already set on the project. Check `Audit → Zcash` reads *up to* a block within a few of the chain tip before you record — if the watcher is behind, payments will not appear.
  - The unit in the demo is **$10**, not the $1,000 the platform defaults to: a batch has to cross a whole unit for anything to move on Solana, and testnet ZEC is scarce enough that a unit the demo cannot reach would show the empty case instead of the mechanism. If asked, say exactly that — production hides behind a large unit, the demo uses one small enough to see.
  - The shielded tab also **prices the crossing live**, both ways, from NEAR Intents' 1Click API: what USDC on Base, Arbitrum or Ethereum becomes in ZEC, and what a ZEC-paid position is worth crossing to USDC on Solana. It needs no key and no account, so it works on any machine with a network — but it is a mainnet price beside a testnet movement, and the panel says so. If the network is down the panel says the price could not be read and everything else still works.
  - **The Solana answer to "can my payment be private?"** After a wallet investment lands, the panel offers a shielded Zcash address for the returns. Worth showing, because the honest half of the answer is on the screen: the deposit is public and cannot be otherwise, and what comes back is what can be shielded. Nothing seeded has it — every demo investment is simulated — so it appears only after the live investment in step 9.
  - Two things to have ready for a question: a swap **lands ZEC transparent** (a shielded recipient is refused), so the shielding is EmpowerFI's step and not the market's; and **Solana is the one chain that market does not route into ZEC**, which is why the way in is from the other three. The way out names whichever chain is pricing it that day — it asked Solana first and will say Base or Arbitrum if Solana is not quoting, which happens.
