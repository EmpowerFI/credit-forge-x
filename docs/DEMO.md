# Demo script: one economic loop

Two scripts, one story. The ninety-second version answers the question the whole product exists for — global capital reached a business, and then what — and needs no wallet, no signature and no live run. The full walkthrough follows the same story end to end: a sponsor's evidence, credit intelligence, a qualified opportunity, capital, repayment, circulation in the territory, and outcome, then back to the sponsor.

It runs on the hackathon environment at `www.empowerfi.io/app`. The login page enters each demo account with one click — nobody types a password on camera.

The bar at the top of the app numbers the three stories: 1 Impact Intelligence, 2 Credit & Capital Engine, 3 Investor Console. Operations sit beside them. On the right, **View platform as** switches between the five views (sponsor, investor or impact fund, operator, community, entrepreneur): each opens a page with that view's value and tools. A demo account switches to the right demo persona in one click, so a judge never has to sign out.

> Prototype of a future regulated P2P productive-credit architecture. Hackathon investments, returns, FX and Pix settlement are simulated; blockchain transactions use test assets on Devnet.

**Optional, after step 12 (30 s):** in **Written in reais, funded in dollars**, **Open the proof** → the **Settlement route** proof, with its routing block: the route, when it was quoted, the net it was quoted at, the provider's rate card, the reason and the comparator's version; then **Verify on Solana**. **Optional, after step 7 (60 s) — the capital network, and the second question:** in the left rail of the engine, **Capital Network**. **Where the capital came from** divides everything the network has already routed. On one side the domestic sources, because domestic capital is not one thing: a regional credit product, a microcredit operator, a productive exchange that is never described as a loan, and people in Brazil lending in reais through the pool. On the other the two routes from abroad, which are not the same money — *investors abroad, share by share*, where nobody has to approve and the raise closes once enough of them have said yes; and *one fund, on its own mandate*, which does have to say yes and asks for reports back. Below the panel, the six routes with the policy each is offered under — ticket, states, purposes, term, reported history, papers — and every route belonging to someone else says *Partner approval required*. "The pool engine asks one question: which of our two pools can fund this. The network asks a wider one — what else exists, and under what terms. It recommends; it never approves."

Then the run the seed leaves for the camera: a request no single pool would take whole. The seed prints its reference at the end of a run, as *left unrouted for the live run*. Its papers are already on file, so the boxes come ticked and nobody types on camera. **Select it → Run the capital engine.** The domestic pool takes what its largest ticket allows and the rest becomes the **external capital gap** — the seed prints the request and the plan prints the two amounts. Under the plan, **Capital from outside Brazil** answers with an amount rather than a yes: the gap, the part of it her month still reaches at the quoted price, and what the plan actually routed abroad. It also shows her cost as the pool engine estimates it beside her cost once a rail has quoted the conversion, and the questions the gap had to pass to get there. "The pool engine answered *waiting for capital*, because R$ 6,700 does not fit inside one pool's ticket. The network splits it instead. And the two kinds of money from abroad part company on this very request: the crowd of investors has less left than its own smallest ticket, so the fund takes the gap. Two answers from one run — the first is what the network can do, the second is asked only of what local capital could not reach."

**What the network refused, and why** carries each refusal with both sides of its comparison: a state a route does not serve, a purpose outside its mandate, a pool whose remaining capacity is under its own smallest ticket — and that last one says where the number comes from, its capital less what it has lent less what the allocation engine has already listed on it. One seeded request is left without its business papers, and its plan shows a local route that a document would reopen. "A refusal a person can check beats one she has to believe, and the fixable ones are the point: this is the list a community leader works from."

**Optional, after step 11 (40 s) — the rail, in detail:** on **Local Economy**, open **How the capital moved** and **The last movements** side by side. Six movements and no others: capital arriving, her buying inputs, one merchant paying another, a merchant buying from her, an instalment, a cash-out. "Three of those are the loop. She buys from a wholesaler, the wholesaler pays a printer, the printer buys from her — and a merchant can only pay with units it actually holds, which is the constraint that makes the multiplier mean anything. Take that constraint away and the number is a setting, not a measurement."

Then the two figures that never merge: **retention**, which is what has not been cashed out for reais, and the **cash-out** itself. "A redemption is the only way off the rail, and counting it as circulation would inflate the multiplier with the exact movement that is leakage. It is the easiest number here to flatter, so it is the first one to check."

And the honest arrangement: three of the four seeded communities have no rail at all. On the **Capital Network**, a plan for a business in one of them carries `LOCAL_RAIL_UNAVAILABLE` where a plan in Grajaú carries `LOCAL_RAIL_ELIGIBLE`. "Her loan disbursed in reais, as it always did. A territory without a rail is a fact about the territory, not a failure of the disbursal — and you can put the two plans side by side."

**Optional, after step 7 (60 s):** **Operating economics** on the engine page. "Can productive credit become cheaper to operate without becoming weaker credit? We don't claim the answer. We measure it: cost to serve beside eligibility discipline, time to decision beside affordability checks, follow-up, and portfolio quality. The rates are pilot assumptions and the data is simulated."

Then **Cost to mobilize capital**, on the same page: what crossed the border, what moving it cost, and the rate per R$ 100 mobilised — set beside the cost to serve per R$ 100 lent, and never added to it. "Here is what it costs to bring that money in, next to what it costs us to serve the loan. Two costs, two denominators, and a hedge that belongs to neither — a required return on currency risk is not a fee paid to a rail. The database is built so the two cannot merge: their rates live in different tables, and no cost event can carry a mobilisation stage." The **eligible external capital gap** on the same panel is what the second question has said yes to and nobody has funded yet; **global funding coverage** is the share of everything lent here that was raised abroad.

**Optional, after step 9 (40 s) — the Zcash track:** the same investment, paid privately, and then handed to the auditor to check without us.

*The payment.* In the invest panel choose **ZEC blindado** instead of **USDC da Devnet**. No wallet is connected and none is needed. The panel answers with EmpowerFI's shielded address, the amount in ZEC at the quote it just locked, a memo carrying a random reference, and a QR. Pay it from any Zcash wallet. The four steps then move on their own: *paid* → *seen in a Zcash block* → *converted to USDC and credited to the vault* → *allocated and proven on Solana*. "She has no Solana wallet, and she does not need one. She pays a shielded address on Zcash. The amount, the memo, the sender and the recipient are all encrypted — and what reaches Solana is the operator crediting the vault, so no investor wallet is ever linked to that inflow."

*The check.* **Operations → Audit → Zcash**. The treasury's **viewing key**, with **Reveal** and **Copy**, and under it the three commands that import it into `zcash-devtool` and list the same payments. "We can read our own treasury because we hold its viewing key. So can an auditor — here it is, and here is how to list these same notes without trusting anything on this screen. It reads everything and can spend nothing. That is selective disclosure, not 'trust our dashboard'."

*If a judge clicks the explorer link and lands on "Transaction Not Found",* that is the demonstration, not a fault, and the screen says so beside the link: a shielded transaction shows no amount, no memo and no addresses, an explorer can only say one exists, and testnet explorers often lag or skip the newest shielded pool. The block height beside it came from our own watcher, which reads blocks and never the mempool — a note it recorded is a note that was in a block.

*Timing.* Testnet block intervals are irregular: a payment has taken 3 minutes and it can take longer. Pay ten minutes before recording and show the finished state, or pay live and cut to the audit console while it confirms. Do not stand in front of the four steps waiting.

## Before recording

- **If a judge asks "is any of this real?"** the answer is a page, not a sentence: the evidence mark in any page header opens **What every number here is made of**. Eleven families of figures, each labelled from its own rows, on the deployment they are looking at. It is worth opening once before recording, because what it says changes as anchors confirm.
- **Choose the language.** From the English site (or with the EN | PT switch) the app reads in English; from `empowerfi.io/pt`, in Portuguese. The demo data is the same in both.
- **Reseed the hackathon environment:** run `seed-demo-accounts.mts`, then `seed-demo.mts --yes`, then `seed-positions.mts --investor-wallet <the wallet you will connect in step 9>`. Together they use about 1.2 devnet SOL.
  - The seed prints the capital figures, the program, the loan it leaves unmeasured for step 16, and the code of the opportunity it leaves **USDC 5 short** for step 9.
  - It also prints the capital network's run: how many plans it recorded, how much gap the second question found eligible, and the request it left **unrouted for the live run**. And it prints **engine cases for step 7** — the three requests that walk the three outcomes — because those amounts move with the scenario and a rehearsed figure that has quietly drifted is worse on camera than no figure at all. Every other request has its papers recorded against it, so the run panel's checkboxes arrive already ticked — nothing on camera has to be typed or restated. One request is left without its business papers on purpose, so its plan shows a local route that a document would reopen.
  - **Two references name one request.** The operator's picker shows a partner the *borrower's* reference (P-…); the investor console shows the *opportunity's* code (Q-…). The seed prints capital-network cases as P-… and investment cases as Q-…, which is what each screen searches by.
  - `seed-positions.mts` admits one wallet per investor and hands each its assets; the mint cron then creates them on devnet, five a minute, so 36 take about eight minutes. Without `--investor-wallet` the wallet you connect in step 9 is not admitted, and the asset in step 13 never appears.
  - Proofs take about half an hour to confirm on devnet after a reseed.
- **Settlement cases** are seeded on global loans and appear in the demo investor's portfolio: two of the direct route, one of the stablecoin winning, one of the stablecoin unavailable. The seed prints them at the end of a run.
- **Case codes** (Q-…) change with every reseed. The engine's picker searches by code, purpose and amount.
- **The local rail** is seeded in one territory only, Grajaú, and the seed prints what it holds: what was injected, what was traded inside the territory, what was cashed out, and the multiplier, retention and velocity — all read back from the Local Economy dashboard rather than counted twice. Check that line after a reseed: if it reports one loan on the rail rather than two, the run came out thin (a settlement provider was unavailable at the moment the book was built) and it is worth reseeding once more.
- **For the Zcash optional:** a Zcash **testnet** wallet with a little TAZ (the faucet gives 0.1 TAZ a day), and the treasury's viewing key already set on the project. Check `Audit → Zcash` reads *up to* a block within a few of the chain tip before you record — if the watcher is behind, payments will not appear.
- **Wallet:** have a devnet wallet with test SOL and at least USDC 5 ready for step 9, and pass its address to `seed-positions.mts`. The invest panel links to the faucets.
- **Check the assets before recording:** open *Positions* as the demo investor. Every asset should show a mint address; if one says "ainda não criado", the mint cron has not reached it yet or the operator wallet is dry — the same wallet the anchor queue uses.
